History

Explore Our Recent Phishing Trends

2021 Year
Microsoft 365 phishing became a major threat, with attackers increasingly using fake Microsoft login pages to steal credentials. Phishing remained heavily focused on account/password theft.
2022 Year
SMS phishing (smishing) and scams targeting banking and cryptocurrency users increased. Attackers increasingly used text messages and fake login/payment pages to trick victims.
2023 Year
MGM Resorts & Caesars Entertainment suffered major cyber incidents involving social engineering. Attackers used impersonation techniques to gain access, showing that human deception remained a major security weakness.
2024 Year
AI-generated phishing became a bigger concern. The UK NCSC warned that generative AI could make phishing messages more convincing and harder for people to recognize.
2025 Year
Phishing continued at very high levels. APWG recorded 1,003,924 phishing attacks in Q1 2025, its highest quarterly total since late 2023. QR-code phishing (“quishing”) and attacks against financial/payment services were notable trends.
2026 Year
Phishing has evolved into more sophisticated attacks involving AI-generated deception, SMS phishing, QR-code phishing, social-media scams, and impersonation.
